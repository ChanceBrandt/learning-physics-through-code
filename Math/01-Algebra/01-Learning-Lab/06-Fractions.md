### Reducing Fractions

**Consider:** $\frac{4}{6}$

1. What common factor do 4 and 6 have? 
    - 2

2. Rewrite both numbers as multiplication using that common factor 
    - 4 = 2(2)
    - 6 = 2(3)

3. You should get something of the form of $\frac{2(2)}{2(3)}$  
Can you see a way to rewrite that so a factor of $\frac{2}{2}$ appears?  
    - $\frac{4}{6} = $\frac{2}{3}$ x $\frac{2}{2}$ 

4. Since $\frac{2}{2}$ = 1, what should be left? 
    - $/frac{2}{3}$ 

### Practice Problem 

**Consider** $\frac{12}{18}$ 

1. What is the greatest common factor of 12 and 18? 
    - 6

2. Rewrite 12 and 18 using that common factor. 
    - 12 = (6)(2)
    - 18 = (6)(3) 

3. Rewrite $\frac{12}{18}$ so that you can visible separate a fraction equal to 1. 
    - $\frac{12}{18}$ = $\frac{(2)(6)}{(3)(6)}$ = $\frac{2}{3}$ x $\frac{6}{6}$ = $\frac{2}{3}$ x 1 

4. What simplified fraction remains 
    - $\frac{2}{3}$

5. In your own words, explain why the value did not change when the fraction was reduced
    - Because we found the greatest common factor between both the numerator and the denominator and formed a fraction using that factor that is equivalent to 1. Therefore, we did not change its value. 


# Definitions

**Numerator:** How many of the pieces 

**Denominator:** The number of pieces the whole is divided into

**Quotient:** The result of division, this can also be a fraction as 12 ÷ 3 = $\frac{12}{3}$

**Equivalent Fractions:** Multiplying the numerator and denominator by the same nonzero factor is really multiplying the entire fraction by 1 so the value does not change.  
    - $\frac{a}{b}$ = $\frac{a*k}{b*k}$ where k is a nonzero factor. 

**Reducing Fractions:** You can reduce fractions by dividing the numerator and denomiator by the same nonzero factor. A common factor in the numerator and denominator forms a factor equal to 1 so it can be removed without changing the fraction's value.
