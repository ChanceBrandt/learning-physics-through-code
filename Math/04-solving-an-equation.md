**Consider:** $2x + 3 = 11$

    - We are trying to discover the value for x that makes the equation true. 
4. If you subtract 3 from both sides, **why does that help?**
    - Subtracting 3 removes the addition of 3 that was being applied to 2x, leaving us with a simpler equivalent equation. 
5. After doing that, you'll have: $2x = 8$ 
    - Why is dividing both sides by 2 allowed? 
        - For the same reason that adding and subtracting from both sides is allowed, as it maintains equivalency on both sides of the equals sign.
6. **Why does dividing by 2 reveal x?**
    - In its current from 2x means that you are taking the variable x and multiplying it by 2.  In order to reveal x, you have to eliminate all mathematical operations applied to x.  Therefore, to remove the multiplication applied to x, you need to do its opposite which is dividing and you need to divide it by the value that it is being multipled by.  Taking 2x and dividing it by 2 means you can cancel or undo the multiplication the 2 and have x by itself. 
    - **Important Vocabulary: Inverse Operations** 
        - Inverse operations are mathematical operations that undo one another. 
            - Addition undoes Subtraction 
            - Subtraction undoes Addition
            - Multiplication undoes Division
            - Division undoes Multiplication

**Consider:** $3x + 5 = 2x + 12$

## What does it mean to solve for x? 
It means to find all values of the variable that make the equation true often by undoing the mathematical operations applied to x using their inverse operations in reverse order to find simpiler equivalent equations that preserve the same solution set until the variable is isolated.
